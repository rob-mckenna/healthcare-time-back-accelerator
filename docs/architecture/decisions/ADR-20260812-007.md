# ADR-20260812-007 — Approval attribution uses opaque actor references

**Status:** Accepted

**Owner:** Trinity

**Requirement IDs:** REQ-APPR-001, REQ-APPR-002, REQ-AUD-002, REQ-DATA-001

## Context

An approval must be attributable: evidence has to answer who accepted a draft and
whether they were authorized. The obvious implementation stores the display name
and email address in the approval event, because that is what a reviewer wants to
read.

That choice puts identifiable personal data into the one record designed to be
retained, exported, and shown in demonstrations. The charter prohibits PII in
this prototype, and attribution does not actually require a name — it requires a
stable reference plus a role.

## Decision

An approval event carries only:

- `actorRef`, an opaque reference matching `^(USR|AGT|SYS)-[A-Z0-9]{8,24}$`
- `roleCode`, checked against `personas.authorizedRoleCodes` in the organization
  pack

No display name, email address, employee number, or free-text identity field is
present anywhere in the approval or audit contracts.

Display names are resolved from the organization pack at render time, for the
screen only. The resolved name is never written back into the event.

The `USR`, `AGT`, and `SYS` prefixes make the actor class explicit, so evidence
distinguishes a human decision from a system-generated event without inference.
Only `USR` actors may record an approval decision.

## Alternatives

- **Store the display name for readability.** Rejected. It places personal data
  in the retained record for a convenience that render-time resolution already
  provides.
- **Store a hash of the email address.** Rejected. A hash of a low-entropy
  identifier is reversible by enumeration and is still personal data, while
  looking as if it is not.
- **Store no actor reference and rely on the platform sign-in log.** Rejected.
  Attribution would depend on correlating two systems, and the charter requires
  the audit event itself to carry the identity reference.

## Consequences

- Approval and audit records can be exported, shown, and retained without
  carrying personal data.
- A display name requires a lookup, so a rendering surface must have access to
  the organization pack.
- Actor reference issuance must be stable per user. In P0 the references are
  synthetic fixtures; a real deployment maps them from a directory identifier
  outside the evidence record.

## Healthcare, privacy, security, and data impact

Positive. It removes staff PII from the retained evidence path entirely, while
preserving the attribution and authorization checks the approval gate depends on.
It also keeps demonstration screenshots free of identifiable staff information.

## Validation evidence

`node scripts/validate-contracts.mjs` — `contracts/schemas/approval-event.schema.json`
sets `additionalProperties: false` and constrains `actorRef` by pattern, so a
name or email field is refused. Fixtures
`approval-event-rejected-without-reason.json` and
`approval-event-missing-reconfirmation.json` confirm the surrounding attribution
rules are enforced. `audit-event-carrying-narrative.json` confirms audit events
cannot absorb identity or narrative content through a spare field.

`node scripts/run-local-journey.mjs` — M5 and M6 in the 2026-08-12 run recorded
an approval bound to the artifact digest after reconfirmation, refused a
wrong-patient reconfirmation, and inspected 22 audit events carrying opaque actor
references and no narrative content.
