# ADR-20260812-003 — The P0 output contract has no recommendation surface

**Status:** Accepted

**Owner:** Trinity

**Requirement IDs:** REQ-SAFE-002, REQ-AGT-003, REQ-STORY-003

## Context

A shift-closeout handoff is, in real practice, adjacent to clinical judgement. A
draft that summarises a shift can drift into "what should happen next" in a
single sentence, and that sentence is a clinical recommendation. The charter
prohibits diagnosis, treatment, triage, and medication recommendation, and more
broadly prohibits clinical recommendation.

The design review raised this as a blocking ambiguity: a "next steps" or
"suggested follow-up" section is natural in a handoff, useful in a
demonstration, and indistinguishable from a recommendation once a model writes
it. A safety boundary that depends on prompt wording is a boundary that fails
silently and only under the interesting inputs.

## Decision

The output contract carries no `recommendation`, `assessment`, `plan`,
`suggestion`, or `nextSteps` property, and sets `additionalProperties: false`.
There is structurally nowhere to put a recommendation, so an over-eager
generation produces a schema failure rather than unsafe content.

The permitted sections are strictly organisational: shift summary, handoff
summary, open items, and follow-up items. Every one of them restates entries
that already exist in the approved synthetic source, each carrying a source
reference that resolves back to that source. `followUpItems` are items already
documented as outstanding in the source record. They are not new items the
generation decided were advisable.

`handoffSummary.pendingHumanDecisionNotice` is a `const` string,
`"PENDING HUMAN DECISION — no recommendation is generated"`, so the absence of a
recommendation is stated positively in the payload rather than inferred from a
missing field.

Any content that cannot be tied to a source reference is a grounding failure
(`E-GROUNDING-FAILURE`), not a lower-confidence paragraph.

## Alternatives

- **Include a recommendation section with a disclaimer.** Rejected. A disclaimer
  next to a recommendation still delivers the recommendation, and the charter
  prohibits the content rather than the confidence level.
- **Include the section but validate it with a downstream classifier.** Rejected
  for P0. It adds an unbounded model-evaluation dependency, and a classifier
  that is wrong once produces exactly the outcome the boundary exists to prevent.
- **Rely on agent instructions alone to suppress recommendations.** Rejected.
  Instruction-only controls cannot be asserted by a validator, so completion
  could not be evidenced.
- **Allow a free-text `notes` field.** Rejected. A free-text field with no
  grounding requirement is a recommendation section with a different name.

## Consequences

- The demonstration shows organisation of documented facts rather than clinical
  insight, which is the honest and defensible story and matches the narrative
  requirement.
- Reviewers can verify the boundary by reading the schema, without reading agent
  instructions.
- A future scenario that legitimately needs an advisory surface requires a new
  ADR and explicit human sign-off. That friction is intentional.

## Healthcare, privacy, security, and data impact

Materially positive. This removes the highest-severity clinical-safety risk in
the slice by making the unsafe output unrepresentable rather than discouraged.

## Validation evidence

`node scripts/validate-contracts.mjs` — fixture
`contracts/examples/invalid/agent-output-recommendation-section.json` is refused
with an `additionalProperties` error, and
`contracts/examples/invalid/agent-output-safety-flag-false.json` is refused for
`noClinicalRecommendation`. The `scope-echo` cross-field rule asserts the
permitted section set matches the requested scope exactly.

`node scripts/run-local-journey.mjs` — M3 in the 2026-08-12 run asserted the
generated draft carries no `recommendation`, `assessment`, or `plan` surface and
that all nine safety assertions hold.
